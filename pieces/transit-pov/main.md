As a New Yorker I feel coddled when I travel and a train tells me which side’s doors will be opening at the next station. I think I first noticed this in Seattle, where the lightrail doesn’t just say the doors will open on _the_ right, but says “doors on _my_ right.”

My findings of how trains refer to their door sidedness:

# NYC MTA subway

_no door side announcements_

## Seattle Link lightrail

“doors on my right”

## Chicago CTA subway

“doors open on the left at [station]”

## CVG Airport People Mover

_no door side announcements_

## Portland MAX lightrail

“doors to the right”

## LA Metro

_no door side announcements_

## Rome Metro

“left side exit”

_[need help transcribing the Italian]

## Further research needed

Please send me more, ideally with direct transcriptions or recordings. Submit a PR to this post if you know that that means, or email me if not.

I am only interested in automated or pre-recorded announcements, unless your agency’s live announcers are especially script-following.

If the train door side announcements are in multiple languages, please capture them all, and help me with non-English transcription if you can.

I’m also interested in taxonomizing:

- station arrivals & departure (“this is,” “now entering,” “we are arriving at,” “you are arriving at,” etc.)
- door open/close announcements and chimes (“doors closing,” “the doors are closing,” “my doors are closing,) etc.)
- self identity (“this train,” “the train,” “we are,” “you are,” etc.)
- buses and other non-trains
